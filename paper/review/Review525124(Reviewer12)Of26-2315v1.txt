This paper presents LLM-Flax, a framework for reducing manual domain
engineering in neuro-symbolic robotic task planning. The method uses
LLMs to generate relaxation and complementary rules from a PDDL domain
file, adds LLM-guided failure recovery, and explores zero-shot LLM
object scoring as a replacement for a trained GNN.

The main strength of the paper is the automatic rule-generation
component. This is a useful and practical contribution. The results
show that LLM-generated rules can match or improve over manually
written rules on the MazeNamo benchmark, and the comparison across
different LLMs provides helpful insight into rule quality and failure
modes. I also appreciate the inclusion of validation, self-correction,
deduplication, and predicate filtering, since these make the approach
more reliable than direct prompting alone.

However, I have some concerns. First, the generalization claim is
stronger than the current evidence. The experiments are limited to
MazeNamo, so it is difficult to conclude that the method generalizes
well to new domains from only a PDDL file. Adding even one additional
domain would make the paper much stronger.

Second, the three stages are not equally supported. Stage 1 is
convincing, but Stage 2 does not appear to improve success rate in the
reported results. Stage 3 is promising but mixed, especially with the
drop on 15×15 Hard. The paper should more clearly separate the strong
results for rule generation from the more preliminary results for
zero-shot object scoring.

Third, the Stage 3 failure case needs more analysis. The explanation
about the 80-fact prompt limit is plausible, but it would help to show
whether important objects are missing, whether irrelevant objects are
scored too highly, or whether increasing the context improves scoring
but hurts runtime.

The paper is generally understandable, but the presentation should be
improved. Some tables are crowded, and the results section tries to
cover many components at once. A clearer organization around the three
stages would help. 

The citations are appropriate and cover relevant work. The related work
could more clearly distinguish this paper from other LLM-for-planning
approaches by emphasizing that this work uses LLMs to generate
structured rule configurations, rather than using the LLM directly as
the planner.

Overall, this is a promising and relevant paper. I recommend revision
to strengthen the evidence for generalization, clarify the limitations
of Stage 2 and Stage 3, and improve the presentation. No multimedia
attachment was available for evaluation.
