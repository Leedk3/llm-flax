The paper uses a local LLM to auto-generate Flax's rule configs (Stage
1), add budget-gated failure recovery (Stage 2), and replace the GNN
scorer with zero-shot LLM scoring (Stage 3). The core idea is
reasonable and I credit the authors for reporting negative results
honestly, but the evidence does not support the claims and the figures
undermine confidence in the work. My detailed comments follow:
1.The title and framing promise "generalizable" / domain-agnostic
planning, but all experiment is on one single domain (MazeNamo).
There's no cross-domain evidence whatsoever, and the authors concede
this is left to future work. The central claim is unsupported.
2. You stated "+0.117 average SR" but exlucing the expert, the gap is
only around 0.03 imrpovement on 7 benchmarks.
3. I found pure FD averages 0.833 above Manual Flax and beats LLM-Flax
on 6/8 benchmarks, yet receives no analysis in the text. A classical
planner with no rule and no GNN is competitive with the full system
almost everywhere, which contradicts the motivation.
4. The deployment claim "only a PDDL file" holds only for Full
LLM-Flax, which is the worst configuration. The configuration that
performs well still needs the GNN trained on 200 problems and is
therefore not deployment-free. You cannot have both good performance
and zero manual effort, and the paper does not resolve this.
5. Stages 2 and 3 might be be considered as contributions. Stage 2 is
explicitly neutral (identical SR, slightly worse time). Stage 3
degrades SR on both tested benchmarks at 4.9 times the cost. Two of the
three stages do not work.
6. The figures are inconsistent and appear to have been generated
without checking against the experiments: Fig 1 says "142/163 objects
pruned" while Figure 4 says 142 retained and 21 pruned; Fig 2 labels
the model "Qwen2.5-14b" but the primary model is Gemma3-12B? FIg 4b
shows the zero-shot scorer outputting a near-constant 0.50 for almost
all objects; Fig 1 caption "full Full LLM-Flax". 
These errors, together with the unexplained baseline anomaly, leave me
unable to trust the reported results.
